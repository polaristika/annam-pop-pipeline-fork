Proprietary practices for quality high yield in mango

<a id='db07bdca-45ef-49d3-8d99-a52e54d80b21'></a>

Although India is the world leader in mango cultivation, the exports of mangoes in our country are low because not only yields are low but also the quality of the fruit is not up to standard.Manashtra ranks second in the country in the production of mangoes.About 68 percent of the total orchard area in Andhra State is under mango plantation.Salina 39 lakh metric tonnes of mangoes are produced in an area of ​​4.71 lakh hectares in the state.Although mangoes are grown in all parts of the state, most of the area is in the coastal districts.Mangoes are extensively cultivated in Krishna, Vizianagaram, West Godavari, East Godavari and Srikakulam districts in the coast, Chittoor and Kadapa districts in Rayalaseema and Karimnagar, Khammam, Adilabad and Nalgonda districts in Telangana.In the coastal districts, the yield and quality of pods have been declining for the past few years.Due to untimely rains during the growing stage of pods, the incidence of pests increases and the pods lose their commercial quality values.

<a id='c44b39f1-34da-42a8-a347-47d9345ecbac'></a>

Reasons for decline in quality yield in coastal districts:

* Mango trees are big (10-20 m. height) and branches are not pruned every year.
* In most of the gardens, the trees are over-aged and in the moment stage.
* Not providing proper nutrients in adequate amounts.
* Using only chemical fertilizers.
* Deficiency of micronutrients especially zinc, boron and magnesium.
* Non-observance of proper water management practices.
* Defects in timely use of recommended medicines.

Pruning:

* Cut the bushy branches of the stem that block the sunlight and ensure that the air and light circulate well inside the tree.
* Dry branches, branches where pests are expected to be cut up to the beginning and apply bordeaux mixture or copper oxychloride to the stems.
* The cut dry branches and dry twigs that have fallen under the trees should be pushed and burnt.
* Pruning of branches should be done in the month of June.

<a id='41433ea6-f036-4038-97fb-a35fa3931d8d'></a>

Comprehensive Fertilizer Ownership:

* Green peas like Jeeluga (10 kg/acre) or Janumu (25 kg/acre) should be mixed with mango trees in the months of June-July with tholakari and mixed in the ground in 45-50 days.
* Organic fertilizers should be used more.Well-rotted cattle dung (about 100 kg) or vermicompost (10 kg) should be applied at the base of each tree.
* After ten years, 1 kg nitrogen (2.2 kg urea), 1 kg potassium (1.6 kg murate of potash) 1 kg phosphorus, (6 kg single super phosphate) fertilizers are applied in June-July at 1.5 m from the main stem of the tree.From 2 m.should be placed in the foot at a distance.
* 50-75 g per tree in plantations with high levels of micronutrient deficiencies.Borax, 125-150 g.zinc sulfate and 125-150 g.Magnesium Sulphate should be applied in June-July months.
* 1.5 - 2.0 g.of zinc sulfate and 1.25 g.Boron (20%) per liter of water should be sprayed to thoroughly wet the trees at the time of new shoot and budding stage.

Ownership of water:

* Watering should be stopped from two days before coating i.e. in the month of October and the trees should be exposed to water stress.This allows the tree to coat quickly and evenly.
* If light moisture is given during the budding stage, it will set the fruit quickly.
* When pods are pea and lemon size, water splashes will increase pod size.
* If watering is stopped 40 days before harvesting the fruit, the quality of the fruit will increase.
